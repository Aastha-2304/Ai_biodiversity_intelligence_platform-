# Land Degradation and Soil Organic Carbon Dynamics

**Authoring Organization**: Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)  
**Report**: Special Report on Climate Change and Land (SRCCL), Chapter 4  
**Publication Year**: 2019  
**Reference**: IPCC SRCCL Chapter 4, pp. 345-436  
**Biome Applicability**: Semi-arid, Arid, Mediterranean, Temperate Grasslands  
**Target Ecological Domains**: Critical SOC Thresholds, Soil Aggregate Stability, Desertification  

---

## Executive Summary & Core Findings

Severe soil organic carbon (SOC) depletion below 0.5% in dryland monoculture agroecosystems triggers non-linear, catastrophic declines in soil structural stability and biological functioning. When SOC drops below this critical threshold:
- **Macroaggregate Breakdown**: Water-stable macroaggregates (>0.25 mm) collapse by over 70%, exposing previously protected carbon fractions to rapid microbial oxidation and physical splash erosion.
- **Plant-Available Water Capacity (AWC)**: AWC is severely restricted; for every 0.1% loss in SOC in sandy loam drylands, soil water holding capacity decreases by approximately 1.5 to 2.2 mm per 100 mm soil profile.
- **Biological Starvation**: Continuous monoculture cereal cultivation devoid of organic residue returns halts the supply of root exudates, triggering widespread collapse of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal (AMF) hyphal networks and soil microbial biomass carbon (SMBC).

### IPCC Consensus Interventions
- **Conservation Tillage + Residue Mulching**: Retaining at least 30% crop stubble cover cuts wind erosion losses by 50–85% and sequesters 0.20 to 0.45 t C/ha/year in semi-arid zones.
- **Diversified Legume Rotations**: Reintroduces rhizosphere microbial diversity and restores drought adaptation resilience with high scientific consensus.
