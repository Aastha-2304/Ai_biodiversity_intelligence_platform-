# Cover Crop Water Use and Cash Crop Yield Trade-offs in Water-Limited Environments

**Authoring Organization**: Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO)  
**Publication Year**: 2021  
**Identifier**: CSIRO Agriculture & Food Technical Report 2021; Agricultural Water Management, 246, 106687  
**Biome Applicability**: Semi-arid, Arid (<350 mm annual rainfall)  
**Classification**: DISSENTING / CONTRAPUNTAL SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE  
**Conflicting Intervention**: Unmanaged / Late-Terminated Cover Cropping  

---

## Dissenting Scientific Findings & Biophysical Trade-offs

While cover crops deliver well-documented soil health and organic matter benefits in sub-humid and temperate zones, empirical multi-year trials across Australia's semi-arid dryland cereal belt (annual rainfall <350 mm) revealed critical hydrological risks:
- **Subsoil Moisture Depletion**: Full-season or late-terminated cover crops transpire between 40 mm and 85 mm of subsoil moisture (0.4–1.2 m depth) during dry springs.
- **Cash Crop Yield Penalties**: Depletion of this stored soil water reduced subsequent wheat grain yields by 8% to 15% during severe drought years due to terminal water stress during anthesis and grain fill.
- **Scientific Caveat / Gating Rule**: In regions receiving less than 350 mm annual rainfall without supplementary irrigation, cover crops must be terminated early (at or before flowering, ~60–75 days after sowing) via roller-crimper or mechanical mowing to retain surface mulch while preserving subsoil moisture reserves for the following cash crop.
