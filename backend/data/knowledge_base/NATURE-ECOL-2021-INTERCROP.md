# Cereal-Legume Intercropping Increases Water-Use Efficiency and Yield Resilience

**Source**: Nature Plants & Ecological Applications  
**Publication Year**: 2021  
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-021-00927-4  
**Biome Applicability**: Semi-arid, Mediterranean, Temperate Grasslands  
**Target Ecological Domains**: Soil Microbial Biomass, Land Equivalent Ratio (LER), Nitrogen Fixation  

---

## Executive Summary & Core Findings

A global meta-analysis of cereal-legume intercropping across water-limited dryland ecosystems revealed that intercropping consistently outperforms continuous cereal monocultures:
- **Land Equivalent Ratio (LER)**: Average LER of 1.28 across 210 field trials, representing a 28% land productivity advantage.
- **Biological Nitrogen Fixation**: Pulse companions (*Cicer arietinum*, *Vigna unguiculata*, *Cajanus cajan*) fix 35 to 75 kg atmospheric N/ha annually, cutting synthetic nitrogen demand and halting soil acidification.
- **Mycorrhizal Fungal Proliferation**: Flavonoid root exudates stimulate arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal (AMF) spore proliferation by +140%, fostering extensive extra-radical hyphae that access microscopic soil pores to transfer capillary water to cereal roots during acute dry spells.
